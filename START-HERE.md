# Start here

Jamie Burkart's work crosses art, civic organizing, public-interest technology,
community infrastructure, cultural policy, and archival systems. This library
is organized around those practices so a reader can enter through a question,
then follow the people, projects, places, and original words that give the work
its meaning.

## Three useful paths

1. Begin with [civic and cultural organizing](library/practices/civic-cultural-organizing/README.md)
   to reach Jamie's public testimony and campaign speech.
2. Begin with [public-interest technology](library/practices/public-interest-technology/README.md)
   to see how open data, documentation, and civic participation join.
3. Begin with [art and critical inquiry](library/practices/art-and-critical-inquiry/README.md)
   to follow the encounter constellations now being recovered.

## What a record means

- `canonical-reference` means the complete public text lives in another
  maintained public source edition and is linked at a pinned commit.
- `public-source-record` means the source is public and the record summarizes
  only what it establishes.
- `public-coverage-gap` means protected research identifies a promising lead,
  but no sufficient public source has yet been recovered for publication.

Nothing here turns an encounter into an endorsement, collective work into sole
authorship, or a photograph into consent.
