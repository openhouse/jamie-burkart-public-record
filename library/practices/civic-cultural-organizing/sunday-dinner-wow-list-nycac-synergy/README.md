# Sunday Dinner, WOW List, and NYC Artist Coalition synergy

## Central finding

Sunday Dinner, WOW List, and New York City Artists Coalition used three
differently scaled containers for a recurring practice: invite people into a
legible situation, help them contribute, make relationships and concerns
shareable, and create a next occasion on which the collective can continue.
Sunday Dinner organized embodied encounter; WOW List organized distributed
discovery and social memory; NYC Artist Coalition organized collective public
agency.

The synergy lies in a reusable pattern of hosting, orientation, list-making,
facilitation, circulation, participatory naming, collaborative editing, and
recurring convening. In this account, continuity is not inevitability.

## Terminology note

This record uses **WOW List**, the project name established across the reviewed
source set. The incoming search term **Wildlist** appears only as one automated
transcript token amid repeated spoken and written references to WOW List. It is
retained as a retrieval term for this synthesis, not asserted as a separate
project or verified historical name.

## Three containers

### Sunday Dinner: recurring encounter

Sunday Dinner created a repeated reason for people to be physically present
together. A hosted gathering can support trust, informal knowledge,
recognition, and serendipity that a directory cannot create by itself. Public
milestone records and a protected aggregate review support a long-running
series at 300-plus scale without publishing participant rows.

Hosting also concentrates real power: invitation, format, atmosphere, place,
and the decision to continue all shape participation.

### WOW List: distributed discovery and memory

WOW List translated some relationships around recurring gathering into
durable, queryable, followable objects. Events, people, places, interests, and
lists could circulate beyond one room and one evening. Facilitators, editors,
guest writers, organizers, and maintainers connected those objects back to
personal invitation and in-person activity.

The [public provenance post](https://x.com/wowlist/status/433671630837919744)
credits Richard Caceres and Jamie Burkart and says WOW List grew from calendars
made at Sunday Dinner. [Shelby's public tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQg47LtixPI)
independently shows an organizer-facing learning artifact.

### NYC Artist Coalition: collective public agency

The public chronology around Call Script, a January 2017 DCLA gathering,
participatory group naming, collaborative letter-making, and a later coalition
meeting shows a shift from distributing cultural activity to coordinating
public action. Participants were invited not only to find an event but to help
name a collective, revise a shared intervention, and return for another
meeting.

The [NYC Artist Coalition public page](https://www.facebook.com/nycartistcoalition/)
and the [Call Script project](https://www.callscript.org/) are public source
pointers, not a complete account of authorship, membership, governance, or
outcomes.

## The reusable social-technical pattern

> invitation → orientation → contribution → shared representation → circulation → convening → continuation

| Function | Sunday Dinner | WOW List | NYC Artist Coalition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation | A recurring hosted gathering | Personal outreach, onboarding, guest-host request, public support | Mobilization into a shared civic-cultural problem |
| Orientation | Host, place, ritual, and encounter | Introductions, explainable recommendations, guides, tutorial | Meeting frame, public questions, naming choices, action context |
| Contribution | Presence, conversation, informal exchange | Add/edit events, make/follow lists, write an introduction | Name a group, edit/sign a letter, join meetings |
| Shared representation | A remembered recurring occasion | Events, lists, profiles, digests, shared notes | Coalition name, event record, collaborative letter, meeting agenda |
| Circulation | Invitations and participant relationships | Embeds, email, notifications, social posts | Call Script, event discussion, participant sharing, advocacy |
| Convening | Return to a room | Discovery aimed toward real-world gathering | Agency gathering followed by a coalition meeting |
| Continuation | Another dinner | Maintained lists, another digest, another guest host | Recurring coalition coordination and public-agency engagement |

The pattern joins social form to information form. A gathering without memory
can dissipate. A directory without relationship can become noise. A coalition
without repeated, legible ways to contribute can become representational in
name only.

## What changed across the transitions

- **Encounter became durable memory:** WOW List made some activities and
  relationships discoverable between gatherings.
- **Hosted recurrence became distributed maintenance:** more people could add
  and tend records, while builders and administrators retained technical and
  governance authority.
- **Discovery became public agency:** the Call Script and NYC Artist Coalition
  record organized people around a public meeting, shared naming, collaborative
  writing, and continued civic action.
- **Belonging became explicit collective identity:** a dinner can cultivate a
  “we” informally; a naming process makes representation a stated question.
- **Cultural circulation became civic address:** event information helped
  people find activity; coalition infrastructure helped a group address a
  public agency.

The public record supports a bounded bridge from WOW List's `popular.vote`
calendar into early coalition organizing. It does not establish that WOW List
caused NYC Artist Coalition.

## Why this synergy matters

Taken together, the projects show Jamie's sustained interest in participation
as an operating system rather than a one-time turnout tactic. The work joins
hospitality, information architecture, product design, facilitation,
communications, and civic organizing. Its strongest qualification is not a
claim that every project succeeded equally; it is experience moving between
embodied, digital, and institutional containers while preserving a next step
for participants.

That individual contribution sits inside collective formation. Richard
Caceres shares public co-builder credit for WOW List. Hosts, guest writers,
editors, organizers, participants, Call Script contributors, coalition
members, and public-agency counterparts also shaped what became possible.

## Boundaries and open questions

- Continuity is not inevitability, sameness, succession, or causation.
- This record does not establish that WOW List caused NYC Artist Coalition or
  that one person founded, represented, or produced the coalition.
- Event-response displays, reposts, database rows, and working-sheet marks do
  not prove attendance, endorsement, mandate, retention, or policy impact.
- Opportunities to participate do not prove equitable participation. Hosting,
  administration, category design, moderation, technology, time, and access
  can distribute power unevenly.
- Public chronology does not completely allocate labor. Collaborator accounts,
  native project records, and early meeting materials could refine the record.
- Visibility is not consent, and a public relation is not permission to infer
  or publish a person's private affiliation.
