# Projects as a recomposable civic-cultural system

## Central finding

Jamie Burkart's projects can be read as context-specific assemblies of smaller
parts: technical modules, social protocols, public artifacts, operating roles,
and stewardship practices. A component becomes reusable when its purpose,
participants, inputs, outputs, decision rights, evidence, credit, maintenance,
and stop conditions are legible. It becomes recomposable when a new project
can adapt those terms rather than merely copying a visible form.

Across Open House, Great Accommodations, Sunday Dinner, WOW List, Call Script,
New York City Artists Coalition, 196 Artists Residency, KC Town Hall, KC
Spaces Fund, and Source-Backed Team Memory, the recurring components include
invitation, hosting, orientation, listening, contribution, shared
representation, circulation, collective decision, public address, resource
routing, documentation, correction, continuation, and handoff.

Code can be forked. A social practice has to be rehosted. In this account,
**adaptation is not duplication**.

## Terminology note

**KC Spaces Fund** is the canonical documented project name. “KC Safer Spaces Fund”

The reading rule is strict: **structural resemblance does not establish historical transmission**.
Chronology and similarity can motivate research, but they cannot replace a
transfer artifact or an attributable account.

## Component model

### Invitation and entry

A handbill, direct message, public listing, event page, application, issue
route, or call script makes a threshold legible. The component includes who can
enter, what they are entering, what is asked, how they may decline, and which
barriers remain.

### Hosting, place, and orientation

A meal, home, gallery, residency, venue, work site, video call, map, guide, or
town hall lets someone arrive. Hosting also holds power over format, attention,
safety, access, timing, and continuation.

### Listening and contribution

Conversation, an event contribution, an edit, survey, resident report,
testimony, name choice, or collaborative text creates a way to affect a shared
object. A contribution route is credible only if someone is accountable for
receiving and responding to it.

### Shared representation

A calendar, list, profile, map, letter, coalition name, proposal, agenda,
funding update, or knowledge record carries situated knowledge beyond one
encounter. It supports coordination while introducing editorial power over
categories, language, visibility, omission, and conflict.

### Circulation and public address

Email, embeds, social publishing, campaign sites, press materials, public
meetings, calls to representatives, and testimony move a shared object toward
another participant or institution. Circulation is not proof of attendance,
endorsement, conversion, authority, or impact.

### Choice and institutional translation

Facilitation, collaborative editing, requirements, public-benefit framing,
proposals, hearings, and compliance records make plural knowledge usable in a
decision setting. The component must distinguish who recommends, decides,
implements, and holds formal authority.

### Resources and material support

Space, independent access, donations, fundraising displays, grant requests,
printing, construction sequences, and service coordination turn attention into
material capacity. Technical, organizer, fiscal, property, grantmaking, and
institutional roles remain separate.

### Memory, maintenance, and handoff

Operating sheets, notes, databases, Git history, source maps, corrections,
meetings, renewals, documentation, and bounded transfers keep work usable after
the first event or launch. They let other components be revised, replaced, or
retired.

## The social interface contract

A reusable component should travel with a packet that states:

1. situated purpose;
2. entry, contribution, refusal, exit, and return paths;
3. inputs and usable outputs;
4. hosting, editorial, funding, approval, maintenance, and stop authority;
5. relational, accessibility, safety, time, language, and material
   preconditions;
6. evidence tier and source basis;
7. collective credit and known absences;
8. custody, privacy, consent, rights, and publication state;
9. maintenance, correction, and handoff; and
10. observable success, unmeasured outcomes, and stop conditions.

This is the social equivalent of an interface definition: implementation can
change while obligations remain reviewable.

## How recomposition operates in community practice

### A pattern is learned through use

Repeated hosting teaches pacing, welcome, orientation, care, and when a room
needs more or less structure. Editorial work teaches how to make a shared
object legible without pretending it is neutral. Campaign work teaches how
public routes meet institutions, deadlines, opposition, and unequal power.

### A new context changes the component

A WOW List tag, coalition name, neighborhood survey, and funded-space spotlight
all represent people and activity, but their risks and decision rights differ.
Recomposition retains a useful function while reopening category design,
access, accountability, correction, and care.

### People carry traditions; artifacts carry partial memory

Collaborators, hosts, participants, organizers, builders, editors, residents,
public servants, and institutional counterparts carry ways of working between
projects. A calendar, form, theme, database, map, letter, meeting record, or
repository can stabilize part of that knowledge. It cannot contain the whole
relationship or grant authority to speak for everyone involved.

### Feedback makes the assembly a system

> encounter → observe → represent → circulate → act → receive feedback → revise → steward or hand off

Recurring dinners, editorial support, coalition meetings, field encounters,
campaign updates, correction paths, and documentation each make a next
iteration possible. Without feedback and an accountable steward, components
become static artifacts.

### Community change remains project-specific

Components can lower the cost of entry, preserve memory, make a concern
shareable, create a route to public authority, direct material support, or
sustain another meeting. Those are mechanisms, not outcome proof. Database
scale, public response, an appropriation, or fundraising result does not by
itself show that one component caused community change.

## When not to reuse

Refuse, stop, or redesign reuse when:

- affected people do not recognize the purpose or decision right;
- participant or relationship records would be exposed without present
  permission;
- a category or workflow would erase language, disagreement, access needs,
  cultural difference, or nonparticipation;
- the new host lacks the trust, safety practice, time, funding, or maintenance
  capacity the form requires;
- technical control would transfer without accountable governance or a
  correction path;
- fiscal, legal, property, public-authority, or institutional roles differ in a
  way the old design conceals;
- only chronology or visual similarity supports the proposed lineage;
- the result cannot be observed honestly or risk exceeds likely benefit; or
- a community already has a form that should be supported rather than
  replaced.

Interoperability includes the ability to refuse an integration.

## Boundaries and open questions

- These projects are not one organization, one product suite, one linear career
  plan, or one person's property.
- **Collective credit** remains project-specific. Richard Caceres shares WOW
  List co-builder credit; NYC Artist Coalition and KC Spaces Fund are wider
  collective formations; the other projects likewise require collaborator
  attribution.
- Only some edges have transfer artifacts. Others remain recurrence or
  structural comparison pending further evidence.
- A participation opportunity is not equitable participation. Hosts, editors,
  administrators, maintainers, funders, property holders, and institutions
  retain uneven power.
- Custody, repository access, public visibility, authorship, rights, consent,
  publication authority, portfolio selection, adoption, and impact are
  separate states.
- No new collaborator testimony was collected for this synthesis. Review can
  add, revise, relabel, or delete proposed edges.
